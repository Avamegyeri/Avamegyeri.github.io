abstract: In collaborative work environments expressed apology plays a critical role in conflict management, cooperation, and trust repair between workers. Robotic assistants in existing collaborative industrial settings, such as warehouses, do not express apology when they make a mistake. In our study, we evaluate four types of robots assisting in a typical warehouse package sorting task\textemdash{}an idle robot that does not assist the package handler if they miss a box and does not apologize, a non-verbal robot that attempts to pick up boxes missed by the package handler but does not apologize for missing the boxes, and two voiced robots that uses neutral voices generated using ElevenLabs to apologize when they are unable to pick up a box that the package handler missed. To enable us to collect data at scale, we design a virtual warehouse where a worker has to remove boxes from a conveyor belt and sort them based on the color of the adhesive tape placed on the box. We design a virtual Universal Robots UR10 arm with a suction gripper to assist the worker in their task in the virtual warehouse. Through our user study with 40 participants, of whom 13 self-identified as Female and 26 as Man/Male, we find that while expressed apology does not impact overall success rates, task performance speed, competence, discomfort, warmth, and trust when working with the robot, there are distinct differences when evaluating how participants self-identifying as male vs. female perceive the robot.
